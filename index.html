02 · Features

Quietly good where it counts.

Each of these exists because playing an album on a phone is still, somehow, worse than it should be.

A wheel, not a list

Playlists sit on a semicircle you spin with a thumb. Two wheel styles, and a search that rotates to the match instead of jumping to it.

Lyrics that keep time

Word level timing from open lyric databases, lined up per singer, with breaks that breathe instead of sitting still.

8D, with a dial

Orbit speed and spatial ambience are yours to set. The mix circles your head as fast or as slowly as you like, and it turns off when you do.

Colour from the cover

Artwork tints the interface, then fades back to black so the music stays the bright thing on the screen.

Artist pages that know something

Open an artist and Coral pulls a short biography and a portrait from MusicBrainz and Wikipedia, so the page is not just a name.

Nothing leaves the phone

Your library is read locally and stays there. No sign in, no sync, no copy in somebody else's cloud.

03 · How it works

Three steps, then music.

No wizard, no import screen, no library to rebuild by hand.

1

Point it at your phone

Coral reads the audio Android already knows about. Nothing is copied, converted or uploaded.

2

Build a wheel

Make a playlist with a name, a cover and the songs you want. It joins the wheel, ready to spin.

3

Spin, tap, play

Drag the wheel to browse, tap the centre to open. The mini player keeps the controls one thumb away.

05 · Questions

The short answers.

Anything else, ask on Telegram and you will get a reply from the person who built it.

What is Coral?

A music player for Android that plays the audio files already on your device. It is built around a playlist wheel instead of a list, with timed lyrics, artist pages and spatial 8D audio.

Is it on the Play Store yet?

Not yet. Coral is in final testing. The build goes to the Telegram channel first, then to the store.

Do I need an account?

No. There is no sign in, no profile and no email required. Open the app and your library is there.

Does it upload my music?

No. Your library is read through Android's media library and never leaves the device. The only network calls are for lyrics, artist biographies and album details, and they ask for a song or artist name, not your files.

Which Android versions work?

Android 10 and newer. The interface, the blur effects and the 8D processor are built for that range.

What file types can it play?

Whatever Android can decode, which in practice means MP3, AAC and M4A, FLAC, OGG and WAV.

What is the wheel?

Your playlists arranged on a semicircle along the left edge. You drag it like a dial and the names rotate past, with the selected playlist large in the centre. There is a simpler style and an advanced one, depending on how much motion you want.

Does 8D audio change the file?

No. It is processed live as the track plays, so the original file is untouched. You can set how fast the mix orbits and how wide the space feels, or turn it off entirely.

Does it work offline?

Playback does, fully. Lyrics, artist pages and album details need a connection because they come from public databases.

What does it cost?

Nothing is charged during testing. If that changes before release, it will be stated here and in the Telegram channel first.
